ABSTRACT

This capstone project proposes the design, development, and quantitative
evaluation of Valencia PowerWatch, a multi-role, web- and mobile-based
community power-interruption reporting, verification, and information
management system for Valencia City, Bukidnon. The proposed system is intended
to provide residents with a structured means of submitting interruption
reports that include location and barangay information, descriptions, and
supporting attachments when available. Authorized administrative and utility
users are intended to review reports, manage incident information and
statuses, publish relevant announcements, and coordinate field assignments.
An authorized staff interface is intended to support field personnel in
accessing assigned work and documenting updates. The system is designed as a
browser-based application with mobile-oriented interfaces, role-based access
controls, and map-supported location presentation. The project will be
evaluated quantitatively using adviser-approved criteria and procedures; the
respondents, instrument, sampling method, and statistical treatment remain to
be specified in the methodology chapter. No evaluation results are reported
in this proposal draft. The system is intended to support organized reporting
and information management and does not independently detect outages, verify
electrical faults, dispatch physical crews, or guarantee restoration times.

Keywords: power interruption reporting, information management, web-based
system, mobile application, geographic information, Valencia City

CHAPTER I
INTRODUCTION

PROJECT CONTEXT

Electricity supports household activities, education, communication,
commerce, public services, and the operation of local institutions. When a
power interruption occurs, residents need timely information about the
affected area, the nature of the interruption, and any available restoration
updates. At the same time, personnel responsible for receiving and acting on
reports need information that is organized, sufficiently specific, and
available in a form that supports verification and coordination.

Reports of power interruptions may reach responsible offices through different
channels, such as telephone calls, in-person messages, or online posts. When
information arrives through separate channels, it may be difficult to
consolidate reports, identify repeated reports concerning the same event, and
maintain a consistent record of the response. A report that does not include
an adequately described location may also require additional clarification
before personnel can assess it. Residents, in turn, may not have a convenient
way to check whether a report was received or whether its status has changed.
These are operational concerns that the proponents must validate with the
intended stakeholders in Valencia City rather than assume to be uniformly
experienced by every office or resident.

Digital reporting and mapping systems offer one way to organize public
observations. A structured form can collect information in a consistent
format, while location fields and a map can help users describe where an
interruption was observed. A centralized information system can make records
available to authorized users, support review of reports, and preserve status
updates. Such a system does not itself restore electricity, determine the
technical cause of an interruption, or replace the authority and procedures of
the local electric utility. It can instead support the reporting and
information-management activities that stakeholders agree are within its
scope.

The proposed Valencia PowerWatch system is intended to provide a shared
digital workflow for community reports and authorized operational users in
Valencia City, Bukidnon. Residents use a mobile-friendly community portal to
submit a power-interruption report, provide a barangay and location, add a
description, and attach supporting images where available. Authorized
personnel can review submitted reports, manage their status, associate
reports with incidents, and coordinate field assignments. The system also
provides an administrative web portal for managing relevant records and
announcements, and a staff-oriented mobile interface for assigned field work.

The system is designed around distinct user roles. Residents submit and
monitor their own reports and view information made available to them.
Personnel accounts are intended for authorized field staff and are limited
according to their assignments and access permissions. Utility and
administrator accounts have additional functions for incident review,
coordination, and system management. These role differences are important
because incident records may contain contact details, precise locations, or
photographic evidence that should not be exposed to every user.

Valencia PowerWatch is a browser-based application with mobile-oriented
interfaces. The community and field interfaces can be opened on a compatible
smartphone browser, and the community interface is implemented as a
Progressive Web App (PWA) that may be installable on supported devices. This
approach provides a mobile-accessible experience without requiring a separate
native application distributed through an app store. The system uses a
server-side application to process requests and a database to organize
records. Its mapping functions use browser-based map technology to present
reported locations and related geographic information.

The rationale for the project is to develop a locally focused tool that
brings reporting, review, status tracking, and response coordination into a
more structured workflow. The project is relevant to the needs of residents
who want to submit an interruption report and authorized personnel who need
to review and manage reports. The system is not intended to make unverified
reports equivalent to confirmed utility events. Verification remains the
responsibility of authorized personnel, and the accuracy of information
depends on the information submitted and checked by users.

The need and suitability of the proposed system should be documented through
consultation with its intended stakeholders. Before final submission, the
researchers should support the local problem context with evidence such as
interviews or written confirmation from relevant offices, an approved
stakeholder needs assessment, or appropriately cited local records. Do not
insert outage counts, response-time measurements, claims about existing
official procedures, or statements that a utility has endorsed the project
unless these have been verified and permission to report them has been
obtained.

PURPOSE AND DESCRIPTION OF THE PROJECT

The purpose of this project is to design, develop, and quantitatively evaluate
Valencia PowerWatch, a multi-role, web- and mobile-based system for community
power-interruption reporting, verification, and information management in
Valencia City, Bukidnon. It is intended to give residents a structured means
of reporting observed interruptions and to provide authorized personnel with
tools for reviewing reports, maintaining incident information, updating
statuses, and coordinating assigned field work.

The community portal is intended to support account access, report
submission, location and barangay details, optional descriptions and
attachments, and access to report and public-information features. The
administrative portal supports authorized users in reviewing reports and
incidents, managing announcements and scheduled-interruption information,
and viewing analytical summaries. The staff interface supports personnel
assigned to field work by providing access to their authorized assignments
and functions for documenting field progress. Access to these functions is
controlled by user role and, for field assignments, applicable assignment
permissions.

The project is intended to organize information and make it easier to follow
a report’s progress. It does not provide a direct electrical-grid control
function, independently verify whether power is available at a location, or
guarantee a restoration time. Any estimates, notices, or status information
presented through the system depend on authorized users and other approved
information sources. The system is a support tool and does not replace
official utility communication channels or emergency procedures.

OBJECTIVES OF THE PROJECT

General Objective

The general objective of the project is to design, develop, and quantitatively
evaluate Valencia PowerWatch, a multi-role web- and mobile-based community
power-interruption reporting, verification, and information-management
system for Valencia City, Bukidnon.

Specific Objectives

The project specifically aims to:

1. Determine and document the reporting, verification, status-monitoring, and
   information-management requirements of the intended resident, field-staff,

   registered residents to submit power-interruption reports with relevant
   barangay and location information, descriptions, and supporting
   attachments where available.

3. Develop authorized administrative functions for reviewing and managing
   reports and incidents, recording status changes, issuing announcements or
   scheduled-interruption information, and viewing system summaries.

4. Develop a field-staff interface that presents assignments to authorized
   personnel and supports the recording of field updates and supporting
   evidence, subject to role and assignment permissions.

5. Apply access controls that distinguish resident, personnel, utility, and
   administrator functions and restrict sensitive report and assignment
   information to authorized users.

6. Evaluate the developed system quantitatively using an adviser-approved
   instrument and evaluation criteria. The criteria may include functional
   suitability, usability, reliability, and performance as appropriate to the
   approved project requirements.

7. Summarize the evaluation results and identify improvements based on the
   measured responses and system tests.

SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS OF THE PROJECT

Scope

The project covers the design, development, and evaluation of a prototype or
pilot information system intended for use in Valencia City, Bukidnon. The
system covers the following principal functions:

1. Role-based account access for residents and authorized operational users.

2. Submission of community power-interruption reports with a selected
   barangay, location information, report details, and optional supporting
   attachments.

3. Review and management of reports by authorized personnel, including
   recording report statuses and associating reports with incident records
   where appropriate.

4. Incident information management, including incident records and relevant
   status updates.

5. Assignment and field-work functions for authorized personnel, including
   access to assigned work, field updates, and supporting evidence.

6. Public-information functions such as announcements and scheduled
   interruption records, when entered and maintained by authorized users.

7. Map-based presentation of report or incident locations and administrative
   summaries based on available system data.

8. Quantitative evaluation of the system using stakeholder responses and/or
   defined system tests, according to the methods to be detailed in Chapter IV.

The system is intended to be accessed through a web browser on desktop and
mobile devices. Its mobile-oriented interfaces are designed for responsive
use, and the community portal may be installed as a PWA on compatible
browsers and devices. Location features depend on device permissions and
location information supplied or confirmed by the user. Attachments depend on
the supported file types, size limits, and storage configuration defined in
the implemented system.

Limitations

The system does not monitor electrical equipment or grid infrastructure
directly and does not independently detect power interruptions. It depends on
reports and information supplied by residents and authorized personnel.
Submitted reports may be incomplete, inaccurate, duplicated, or delayed;
therefore, a report must not be treated as an officially verified outage
without review by an authorized person.

The system does not dispatch physical crews automatically, control power
distribution equipment, guarantee a repair or restoration time, or replace
the electric utility’s established operational procedures. Actual response
and restoration decisions remain with the responsible utility and its
authorized personnel. Any notification, schedule, status, or estimate is only
as current and accurate as the information entered and maintained in the
system.

Report submission and access to current server information require a working
network connection. Although a PWA can cache selected application resources
for loading, the current project documentation does not establish a verified
offline queue for outage reports; the proposal therefore does not promise
that reports will be submitted automatically after reconnecting. Map tiles,
location lookup, external services, and current records may also be
unavailable when the relevant network or service is unavailable.

The system’s coverage is limited to the functions and user groups approved
for this project. Integration with a utility’s operational systems, feeder
and transformer databases, official outage-management platforms, SMS
gateways, native Android or iOS applications, and automatic grid telemetry
are outside the scope unless separately approved and implemented. The
researchers must confirm the final feature list against the version evaluated
for the capstone.

The quantitative evaluation will be limited to the participants, instruments,
time period, and test conditions approved in Chapter IV. Unless a
representative sampling design and sufficient evidence are established, the
results should not be generalized to every resident of Valencia City or used
to claim measured improvements in actual utility restoration time.

RELATED THEORIES

DeLone and McLean Information Systems Success Model

DeLone and McLean (2003) updated an information-systems success model that
describes interrelated dimensions commonly used to assess an information
system. The updated model includes system quality, information quality,
service quality, use or intention to use, user satisfaction, and net benefits.
The model is relevant to Valencia PowerWatch because the system is intended
to provide several user groups with usable functions and information that
supports reporting and coordination.

For this project, system quality may be considered through such observable
characteristics as whether the required functions work, whether the system is
available under the test conditions, and whether the interfaces can be used
on supported devices. Information quality may be considered through the
completeness, clarity, and usefulness of report fields and status information.
User satisfaction and perceived benefits may be measured through responses
from the approved participants. The model does not mean that the researchers
can claim a community-wide reduction in outages or restoration time merely
because the software is available. Such outcomes require suitable operational
data and an evaluation design capable of establishing them.

The model informs the project’s proposed evaluation by encouraging the
researchers to assess more than the existence of software features. The
instrument and operational definitions should be aligned with the approved
research questions, and the results should be reported only for the
dimensions actually measured.

Technology Acceptance Model

Davis’s (1989) Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) focuses on perceived
usefulness and perceived ease of use as important factors associated with
users’ acceptance of information technology. Perceived usefulness concerns
whether a person believes that using a system helps accomplish relevant
tasks. Perceived ease of use concerns the degree to which the system is
perceived as requiring little effort.

TAM is relevant because Valencia PowerWatch is intended for users with
different responsibilities and levels of familiarity with digital tools. A
resident must be able to understand the report form and communicate a
location, while authorized staff must be able to locate assigned records and
record updates. If the system is difficult to use or does not support the
users’ tasks, intended functions may not be used consistently. For this
reason, a quantitative evaluation may include clear, adviser-approved
questions about ease of use and perceived usefulness.

TAM is used here as a conceptual guide, not as proof that all residents will
adopt the system. Actual acceptance depends on users, access to compatible
devices and connectivity, awareness, organizational practices, and other
conditions. The researchers should identify the specific TAM constructs
included in the study and avoid extending conclusions beyond their data.

Synthesis of Related Theories

The DeLone and McLean model provides a broader view of information-system
quality, user experience, and benefits, while TAM focuses on users’
perceptions of usefulness and ease of use. Together, these theories support
an evaluation that examines both how well the system functions and how users
perceive its role in their tasks. They also provide a rationale for including
quantitative user feedback in the evaluation. The final evaluation model,
instrument, and analysis must be confirmed by the adviser and described in
Chapter IV.

RELATED PROJECTS

Open311 and GeoReport

Open311 is an open standard and ecosystem for structuring civic service
requests and interactions; it is not a single ready-made reporting
application. Its model illustrates how a public user can submit a service
request through a client while an organization receives and manages the
request through a server connected to its records. The Open311 community
describes the standard as supporting interoperability among applications
used for service requests and citizen interactions (Open311, n.d.; mySociety,
2013).

The project is relevant because it demonstrates the value of treating public
reports as structured service requests rather than unorganized messages.
Valencia PowerWatch similarly organizes resident reports for review by
authorized personnel. However, the proposed system is tailored to power
interruption reports, role-based field assignments, and local status
management; it is not represented as Open311-compliant unless such
compliance is separately implemented and tested.

City of Bloomington uReport

The City of Bloomington’s uReport is an open-source constituent relationship
management web application with an Open311 GeoReport v2 endpoint. Its project
description identifies it as a lightweight constituent-management tool for
municipalities and describes feeds that can be integrated with existing
environments (City of Bloomington, n.d.). This project is related to
Valencia PowerWatch because both involve receiving and organizing
community-submitted service issues for an administrative workflow.

The uReport project illustrates a municipal approach to structured
constituent reports and integration. Valencia PowerWatch differs in its
specific focus on power interruptions, its resident and field-staff
interfaces, and its incident and assignment workflow. The comparison does
not imply that either project is used by Valencia City or that uReport
provides the same power-outage functions.

ArcGIS Survey123

ArcGIS Survey123 is Esri’s form-centric solution for creating and sharing
surveys, collecting information through web or mobile devices, and analyzing
results. Its documentation describes location-aware forms and the ability to
capture text and images; it also describes mobile data collection in online
and offline conditions (Esri, n.d.). These characteristics are relevant to
the design of structured report forms and field data collection.

Survey123 is a general data-collection platform rather than a system
specifically designed for the proposed Valencia power-interruption
workflow. Valencia PowerWatch focuses on community outage reporting,
verification, incident status, authorized field assignments, and public
information in one locally oriented application. Its current report
submission must not be described as having Survey123-style offline
submission unless that function is separately implemented and verified.

RESEARCH GAP AND PROJECT RELEVANCE

The reviewed theories provide bases for evaluating system quality and user
acceptance, while the related projects demonstrate approaches to structured
civic reports, municipal case management, and mobile data collection. The
reviewed sources do not establish the existence of a locally tailored
multi-role community power-interruption reporting and field-information
workflow for Valencia City. That statement is a project rationale and must
be checked through a documented local needs assessment and a more complete
literature search before final defense.

Valencia PowerWatch addresses this proposed gap by focusing on information
that can be collected from community reports, reviewed by authorized users,
and maintained through report, incident, and field-work functions. Its
expected value is to support better organized information and communication
within its approved scope. Whether users find it useful, easy to use, and
functionally suitable must be determined through the actual quantitative
evaluation rather than assumed in advance.

TECHNICAL BACKGROUND

This chapter describes the technology areas relevant to the development and
operation of Valencia PowerWatch. It follows the supplied capstone guide by
discussing hardware, software, peopleware, and network requirements. The
description distinguishes the current prototype implementation from
functions or hosting arrangements that still require verification.

OVERVIEW OF THE SYSTEM TECHNOLOGY

Valencia PowerWatch follows a client-server approach. Users access a
browser-based interface from a desktop or mobile device. The interface sends
requests to a server application, which applies authentication and role
permissions, processes reporting or management operations, and reads or
updates stored records. The server returns information for presentation in
the relevant portal. Map views use geographic coordinates and browser-based
mapping components to display locations.

The system has separate community, administration, and field-staff
interfaces. They share the server-side application and core information
records, but the functions presented to a user depend on the user’s role and
authorization. Community reporting and information access are distinct from
administrative review and personnel assignment functions. This separation is
important for usability and for limiting access to sensitive data.

HARDWARE

Client devices. Residents may access the community interface using a
smartphone, tablet, laptop, or desktop computer with a supported web browser.
Mobile devices should have a working display, browser, and location service
if the user chooses to provide a GPS-based location. A user may need to
select or confirm a location manually when device location is unavailable or
inaccurate. Field personnel require a compatible mobile device to view
authorized assignments and record field updates.

Administrative devices. Administrative, utility, and dispatch users may use
a desktop or laptop computer with a modern browser for reviewing reports,
managing records, and viewing map or summary interfaces. A larger display
may be more convenient for information-dense administrative functions, but
the final minimum screen and device requirements should be determined from
testing.

Server and storage. The application requires a computer or hosted server
environment capable of running the supported Node.js server process and
storing application data and authorized file attachments. In the current
prototype, the operational application database is SQLite and uploads are
stored as files. For the planned hosted system, the researchers have selected
Supabase PostgreSQL as the target database and Supabase Storage for private
evidence files. This migration has not yet been implemented or verified, so
these services must be described as planned rather than operational. The
availability and durability of the current and planned storage depend on the
configured host, service plan, and backup arrangements.

SOFTWARE

Client-side application. The user interfaces are implemented with HTML,
CSS, and JavaScript for modern browsers. The community interface is
mobile-oriented and uses PWA-related components, including a web app
manifest and service-worker resources. A PWA can provide an installable
browser-based experience on supported devices; it is not the same as a
native application distributed through the Android Play Store or Apple App
Store. Cached application files do not mean that authenticated records or
new reports are available offline.

Server-side application. The prototype uses Node.js with the Express web
framework to serve the portals and application programming interfaces
(APIs). The server receives requests, validates relevant inputs, checks
authentication and role permissions, and performs operations required by
report, incident, announcement, scheduled-interruption, analytics, and
field-work functions. The supported Node.js version is specified by the
project’s software configuration and should be confirmed against the
deployment used during testing.

Database and file handling. The current prototype uses SQLite for
application records such as accounts, reports, incidents, assignments,
announcements, and related history. Supporting images and other allowed
attachments are handled separately as uploaded files. Access to uploaded
evidence is restricted through application checks. The codebase also
contains a Neon PostgreSQL integration intended for resident authentication;
its connection and hosted operation have not been verified for this draft.

For the planned hosted system, Supabase PostgreSQL has been selected as the
target relational database for resident and staff accounts, barangays,
reports, incidents, assignments, announcements, notifications, and activity
records. Supabase Storage is the planned location for private evidence
photos and other allowed files, while the database would retain file
references and metadata. The existing Node.js and Express backend is
intended to remain responsible for application workflows and role-based
access checks. Migration from SQLite and the existing Neon integration is
pending; therefore, this draft does not claim that Supabase is configured,
connected, or operational. The migration must preserve existing records and
account access and be verified through backups, data checks, and workflow
tests before the deployed system is described as using the target services.

Mapping and external services. Leaflet is used for browser-based interactive
maps. Map presentation may depend on the availability of map tiles and
internet access. Location features depend on the browser’s permission model,
the device’s location capability, and the accuracy of the selected
coordinates. The prototype also requests weather information from an
external weather service; such information is dependent on network access
and the external service and should not be treated as an authoritative
electric-utility status source.

Security and testing. Authentication, role-based authorization, server-side
validation, and restricted access to report evidence are relevant software
controls. These controls should be evaluated with functional tests and
authorized-user scenarios. Testing should use a development or test
environment and test accounts rather than altering real production records.
The evaluation should verify the implemented version and report any
remaining defects or limitations.

PEOPLEWARE

Peopleware refers to the people who use, administer, support, and maintain
the system. The intended user groups are:

1. Residents, who create accounts, submit reports, and view information
   available to them.

2. Field personnel, who access work assigned to them and record field
   progress or evidence according to their permissions.

3. Utility or dispatch users, who review operational information and perform
   authorized coordination functions.

4. System administrators, who manage user access and system-level records
   within their approved responsibilities.

5. Project researchers and technical maintainers, who configure, test,
   document, and maintain the system during the project.

Successful use depends on users understanding what information to provide,
what the reported statuses mean, and which official channels remain
authoritative. Personnel responsible for verification should be trained to
review reports consistently and protect personal information. The system
does not replace human verification or operational judgment. The researchers
should identify the actual stakeholder roles and any required training
through consultation before finalizing the deployment plan.

NETWORK

The system uses a client-server network in which a browser communicates with
the application server over HTTP or HTTPS, depending on the environment.
When hosted for public access, HTTPS should be used to protect data in
transit. User actions such as signing in, submitting a report, viewing
current records, and uploading attachments require communication with the
server. Residents and field personnel therefore need internet or mobile-data
access for live use.

The system depends on the availability of the client’s connection, the
application host, database and file storage, and any external map or weather
services used by the interface. A slow or unavailable connection can delay
responses or prevent report submission. Mobile coverage may vary by
location. The interface may retain selected static application resources
under PWA rules, but current server data and report submissions require
connectivity in the version described here. A future offline-report feature
would require separate design, secure local storage, conflict handling,
reliable retry behavior, and testing before it could be included in the
system’s stated capabilities.

CURRENT TRENDS AND TECHNOLOGIES RELEVANT TO THE PROJECT

Progressive Web Apps provide a way to deliver installable, responsive
browser-based experiences using web standards. For this project, a PWA
approach can make the community portal accessible on supported mobile
devices while maintaining a shared web application. It should not be
confused with complete offline operation: offline capability must be
specified and tested for each type of data and action.

Geographic information systems and web mapping support the visualization of
reported locations. A map can help a user identify or confirm the location
associated with a report and help authorized users review spatial patterns.
The visual map is only as reliable as the coordinates and underlying data.
An approximate or user-selected point should not be interpreted as a
surveyed boundary, electrical network asset, or confirmed fault location.

Role-based access control is relevant to multi-role applications because
residents, field staff, utility personnel, and administrators have different
tasks and data needs. Permission checks should be enforced by the server,
not solely by hiding interface controls. Authentication protects access to
accounts, while authorization determines which records and actions an
authenticated user may use.

Quantitative system evaluation supports a measurable assessment of the
prototype. The researchers may collect structured participant responses and
conduct defined functional or performance tests. The particular measures,
sampling plan, instrument validation, statistical treatment, and criteria for
interpreting results must be set out in Chapter IV and approved before data
collection. No numerical performance or user-acceptance result is asserted
in this technical-background chapter.

TECHNOLOGY SUMMARY

The current prototype uses HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for its user interfaces;
Node.js and Express for its server and APIs; SQLite as its operational
database; and Leaflet for interactive maps. The community interface also
uses PWA manifest and service-worker resources. Internet access and HTTPS
are required for live portal and API use.

Supabase PostgreSQL and Supabase Storage have been selected as the target
services for hosted relational data and private evidence files. Their setup
and migration are pending, so they are planned technologies rather than
verified operational components. Residents, staff, utility personnel, and
administrators are the intended users. The final manuscript must match the
system version and configuration actually used for the capstone evaluation.
